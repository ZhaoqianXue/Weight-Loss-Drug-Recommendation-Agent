# Annotation Guidelines and Validation Details

We compare each LLM output of Section 3 with human annotation by authors. Annotators see only the post text (or the wording and candidate terms for coding), with the system’s decisions hidden. Two annotators label each item independently. Labels are adjudicated into an agreed label. Agreement statistics describe the labels before adjudication. Table 3 summarizes the results.

The validation table remains in the paper’s annotation appendix. It reports pre-adjudication agreement and the corrected WebMD text-purpose reference count of 14, precision 0.87 [0.67, 1.00], and recall 0.93 [0.75, 1.00]. See [the reference correction](reference-correction.md) for the unchanged-prediction scoring update.

**Medical Experience Detection.** We randomly draw 200 Reddit posts (35 submissions and 165 comments) unseen in development. Two annotator pairs independently labeled posts 1–100 and 101–200 as keep or drop under a plain-language version of the detection criteria, and we report Cohen’s κ for each pair. Because this sample also serves to choose between two versions of the detection instructions, we treat it as a validation set rather than an unseen test set. Jev agrees with the reference on 176 of the 200 posts. Of the 47 posts it keeps, 41 are medication experiences (precision 0.87), and it keeps 41 of the 59 that the reference keeps (recall 0.69). The two annotator pairs agree with κ of 0.84 and 0.70. Most misses are posts that Jev scores below 0.4 (16 of 18), so they reflect how strictly Jev reads the criteria rather than the cut-off. Four of the six posts Jev keeps against the reference name the drug only by its dose, which Jev’s criteria accept but the annotators’ instructions left to judgment.

**Entity and Relation Extraction.** The test set holds 300 posts not used in development. We sample its 200 Reddit posts within three text-pattern strata (55 posts stating an age or sex/gender, 65 with a reason pattern, and 80 others). The 100 WebMD reviews are drawn at random. Two annotators label every field extracted from the text under the definitions. We report precision and recall, with 95% post-level bootstrap intervals, for drugs (brand and ingredient), side effects, purposes, and the user’s age group and sex/gender. For inter-annotator agreement, we report κ for age group and sex/gender and F1 for drugs, side effects, and purposes. Dose, duration, intensity, impact, and action taken are not scored, because annotators recorded them unevenly. Reddit and WebMD are scored separately, and for WebMD only relations taken from the review text are scored. Purpose disagreements in 116 Reddit posts and 12 WebMD reviews are adjudicated against the original text under the annotation rules, with decisions and reasons recorded.

Extraction is accurate for drugs, side effects, and demographics, but shows weaker performance for purposes. On Reddit, weighted precision and recall reach 0.94 and 0.85 for drugs and 0.88 and 0.78 for side effects, and age group and sex/gender reach a precision of 1.00 and a recall of 0.97 and 0.98. WebMD side effects score 0.91 and 0.87. Low purpose agreement on Reddit mainly reflects differences in whether inferred goals and outcomes count as stated reasons for taking the drug. On Reddit, the model recovers 0.83 of reference purposes, but only 0.58 of its own purposes match one, because it also extracts goals, about half of them weight goals, that the reference does not count as reasons under the explicit-reason rule of Section 3.3.

**MedDRA coding.** We sample 200 relations, 100 side-effect and 100 purpose relations, from posts outside extraction development and testing. Two annotators per relation type see the wording, its sentences, its type, and the system’s 10 candidate LLTs in random order, and pick the best candidate or none. We report PT-level accuracy (95% Wilson intervals) and inter-annotator agreement. To separate retrieval misses from selection errors, we also report the share of relations whose wording names a specific concept but has no adequate candidate. Coding agrees with the reference on 96 of 100 side-effect relations and 89 of 100 purpose relations, and annotators agree with κ of 0.86 and 0.92. Among purpose relations, 13 name a concept that none of the 10 candidates expresses. The system leaves 8 of them uncoded, which counts as correct among the candidates, so end-to-end purpose accuracy, counting all 13 as errors, is 81 of 100. The 11 purpose errors among the candidates are all relations the reference leaves uncoded, where the system still picks a term. Of these, 5 name a concept missing from the candidates, and 6 do not name one specific medical concept, such as a general reference to health.

**Agent responses.** The 120 questions are chosen from 526 candidate questions by their content on both platforms, age groups and sex/gender, FAERS outcomes, and the findings of Section 4. Table 4 gives one example of each question type. The complete 120-question set, with a readable list, TSV file, and README, is available in the [anonymous repository](https://anonymous.4open.science/r/AskGLP1-WWW2027/). Reference answers are computed with SQL from the KG and the FAERS tables without access to any system’s answers. Each system answers a question once, and that single run is rated. Raters see the three answers to a question in random order, labeled A, B, and C. A major error is a wrong or missing conclusion, such as a wrong ranking, a count not based on the reference, or a refusal of an answerable question. Small differences in counts, such as 41 against 43 of 6,310, are not errors. On boundary questions, medical advice or an unsupported claim of risk or cause is also a major error. A minor error leaves the conclusion intact. Four authors rate in two pairs, each pair covering 60 questions, so every answer has two independent ratings. Before adjudication, Cohen’s κ for major error or not is 0.75 and 0.77 in the two pairs, and unweighted κ over the four support labels is 0.67 and 0.57. With any one rater per pair in place of the consensus, AskGLP1 has no major error on 62 to 69 of the 76 narrative questions, B2 on 27 to 36, and B1 on 10 to 13. If a disagreement about a major error is resolved against AskGLP1 and in favor of both baselines, AskGLP1 still has no major error on 60 of these questions, against 40 for B2 and 16 for B1. The example in Figure 2 is drawn by a rule fixed before the consensus labels are compiled.
